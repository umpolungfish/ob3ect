The canonical frame is the one the boundary marks name

A word opens on VINIT and closes on TANCH. That is the convention, and it means
the canonical rotation is not a matter of taste: the cut putting ⊢ first and ⊣
last is the word's own frame.

The audit was picking `min(orbit, key=word)` — lexicographic, which is by
CODEPOINT, and ∈ (U+2208) sorts below ⊢ (U+22A2). So it landed on a cut that opens
mid-word and buries both boundaries inside the ring. On the carrier ob3ect it
reported canonical_k = 1 and a canonical_word of ∈≻⊤⊥≺…⊣⊢, disagreeing with the
artifact's own glyph_word for no reason a reader could see, and inviting exactly
the wrong conclusion — that glyph_word was an arbitrary frame and the canonical
one was considered.

It was the other way round. glyph_word was right.

Now the canonical frame is the bounded one, with the lexicographic representative
kept only for a word carrying no boundary to read — which is the case the
convention genuinely leaves open. On the carrier word this returns k = 0 and a
canonical_word identical to glyph_word.
