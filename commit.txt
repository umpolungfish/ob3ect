I made the ob3ect ouroboricity tier honest — the Grammar computes it, I do not guess it.

Every ob3ect was stamped with a tier (O₀/O₁/O₂/O∞) on its wiring diagram, in its Lean
scaffold, and in its JSON. That tier came from three shallow heuristics that never looked
at the object's imscription — they checked only whether the first and last bootstrap tokens
matched and whether a Frobenius pair was present. They inflated nearly everything to O₂,
one to O∞.

I routed every emitter through the Grammar's own verdict: proof_scaffold.ouroboricity_tier,
which is cl8nk_navigator.assess_tier scored on the 12-primitive tuple the opcodes
procedurally imscribe. One source of truth, no proxy, no hand-supplied tuple. I keep the
tier deferred to the Grammar precisely so my bias never enters it.

Fixed at source: emit_scaffold, auto._generate_diagram, batch_diagrams.tier_from_ops, and
the scaffold wrapper's canonical-class readout. Then I added retier.py and regenerated every
carrier for all existing objects — diagrams, pen diagrams, scaffolds, and the stored JSON
scaffolds — plus an orphan sweep for the tracked flat-dump copies under nodraw/ and
zoom_cosmos/. Two special cases I fixed by hand: the binance py-module scaffold and a stale
betting diagram with no matching JSON.

The honest verdict is O₀ and O₁ only. The opcode-to-field map can drive just three of the
eight tier-scoring primitives — criticality, winding, and recognition — to their terminal
values, so O₁ is the true ceiling for anything built from the current opcodes. That is the
Grammar being self-diagnostic, and I let it stand.
