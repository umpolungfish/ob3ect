Reinforce auto.py with what the efl_affirm/efl_negate pair just taught it

Ran the affirmation and negation of a corrected Erdős-Faber-Lovász through the
auto-designer. The negation closed clean, one fork, one fuse, nothing
discarded. The affirmation carried an orphan FSPLIT at its very first step,
"branch the construction process into the generation of n distinct cliques" -
a fork with nothing on the other side of it, because building n cliques isn't
a decision, it's a repetition. insert confirmed it: already holds, nothing to
repair, because nothing was ever at risk there.

Traced it to the schema. Rule 4 says every FSPLIT wants a matching FFUSE, but
the TOPOLOGICAL DIVERSITY section tells the model not to default to flat
chains and offers open forks as a legitimate topology choice. Nothing in
between says what actually distinguishes a genuine either/or from an iterated
construction, so the same "build n things" instruction can read as a fork or
a chain depending on phrasing alone. Added a DECISION VS ITERATION paragraph
naming the difference directly, with the vacuous/already-holds outcome as the
tell that a fork wasn't a real one.

While tracing the fork with insert, found that _kernel_repairs was silently
losing the two most informative outcomes: "already holds, nothing to repair"
and "exposed, N units cleared with nothing banked" both fell through the old
three-way check (holds/counts/vacuous) into a plain None, indistinguishable
from the kernel call producing nothing at all - and vacuous alone, without an
accompanying holds list, hit the same silent None. Fixed the parser to catch
all of it and added a report line so it surfaces in the printed artifact
block too, not just the JSON.
